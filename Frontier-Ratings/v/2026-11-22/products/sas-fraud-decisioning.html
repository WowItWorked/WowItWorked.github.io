Fraud detection and prevention product on the SAS Viya platform that profiles and scores transactions and customer events in real time with rules and machine-learning models, and supports alert triage, investigation and case management. SAS presents it at the web address formerly used for SAS Fraud Management. Anti-money-laundering monitoring is sold as a separate product, SAS Anti-Money Laundering.

How confidence is setIt follows the kind of evidence, not judgement.
  • The customer's own statement (high confidence at most): The organisation says so itself: its press release, website, annual report or a named executive on the record.
  • Regulatory or securities filing (high confidence at most): A filing by the customer or the vendor names the relationship.
  • Independent press (high confidence at most): Editorially independent reporting describes the deployment.
  • Joint announcement (medium confidence at most): A press release issued with the vendor, usually with a quote from the customer.
  • Vendor case study (medium confidence at most): The vendor's published customer story naming the organisation.
  • Vendor mention only (low confidence at most): A logo, a customer list or a passing mention by the vendor, with nothing from the customer.
  • An organisation that also holds a stake in the vendor is labelled “Investor and customer” and is never rated above medium.
  • A partnership is listed as a partner, not a customer.

Questions to ask the vendor (12)

Each is something that could not be established from public sources. Export them as a list to send, with empty columns for the vendor’s answer and the evidence supplied.

Export and share
Open as a question list
  1. How many people does SAS employ, and how many work in engineering and in support for the SAS Viya platform?
    Headcount indicates the capacity to support a large installed base.
  2. What was SAS's revenue and operating profit in its last financial year, and can audited accounts be shared under NDA?
    Revenue shows the scale of the business behind the product; SAS is private and publishes no audited figure.
  3. How many organisations license SAS Viya, and how many of them are banks?
    Customer numbers indicate how widely the products are used.
  4. Will SAS provide audited financial statements for the last three years under NDA?
    Without published accounts a bank cannot verify the vendor's financial condition itself.
  5. Have any banks ended or materially reduced their use of SAS Viya, SAS Model Risk Management or SAS fraud products in the last three years, and why?
    Loss of large customers can signal product or service problems.
  6. Has SAS had any executive departures for cause, governance disputes or regulatory findings against officers in the last three years, and what is the succession plan for the chief executive?
    Leadership stability affects delivery and support.
  7. Does SAS Fraud Decisioning use data or models pooled across institutions, and if so who contributes and how is the data governed?
    Pooled intelligence can detect fraud a single bank cannot see.
  8. When did SAS Fraud Decisioning become generally available, how does it relate to SAS Fraud Management and SAS Financial Crimes Analytics, and which banks run it in production?
    A bank needs to know how long this product has been in production and at whom.
Show the other 4
  1. What does SAS Fraud Decisioning log for each decision and each rule or model change, how long is it kept, how can it be exported, and which SIEM products are supported?
    Fraud decisions must be reconstructable for disputes, auditors and supervisors.
  2. How is transaction and customer data encrypted in SAS Fraud Decisioning, and can the customer manage the keys?
    Encryption and key ownership determine who can read payment and customer data.
  3. Are the prebuilt fraud models in SAS Fraud Decisioning trained on other customers' data, and is our data used to train models that serve anyone else?
    A bank must know whether its transaction data trains models used by other institutions.
  4. What availability has the SAS-managed service delivered, what availability and latency commitments are offered, and how are rules, models and case history exported at exit?
    Real-time fraud scoring sits in the payment path; outages and exit both carry direct cost.
