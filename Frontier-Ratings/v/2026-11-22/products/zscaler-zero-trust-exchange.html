Cloud-delivered security platform that sits inline between users, devices, workloads and the applications they reach. Its core services are Zscaler Internet Access (secure web gateway with TLS inspection, firewall, data loss prevention and CASB), Zscaler Private Access (zero-trust access to private applications) and Zero Trust Branch for sites. The vendor says it runs in more than 200 of its own data centres.

How confidence is setIt follows the kind of evidence, not judgement.
  • The customer's own statement (high confidence at most): The organisation says so itself: its press release, website, annual report or a named executive on the record.
  • Regulatory or securities filing (high confidence at most): A filing by the customer or the vendor names the relationship.
  • Independent press (high confidence at most): Editorially independent reporting describes the deployment.
  • Joint announcement (medium confidence at most): A press release issued with the vendor, usually with a quote from the customer.
  • Vendor case study (medium confidence at most): The vendor's published customer story naming the organisation.
  • Vendor mention only (low confidence at most): A logo, a customer list or a passing mention by the vendor, with nothing from the customer.
  • An organisation that also holds a stake in the vendor is labelled “Investor and customer” and is never rated above medium.
  • A partnership is listed as a partner, not a customer.

Questions to ask the vendor (11)

Each is something that could not be established from public sources. Export them as a list to send, with empty columns for the vendor’s answer and the evidence supplied.

Export and share
Open as a question list
  1. Which large customers have left Zscaler or materially reduced their use in the last two years, and why?
    Loss of large customers can signal product or service problems before they show elsewhere.
  2. What administrator audit logging does the Zero Trust Exchange provide, how long is it retained, and can it be exported?
    Banks need a record of administrator changes to security policy.
  3. For any allowed or blocked transaction, can the platform show which policy rule and which threat or data verdict produced the decision?
    A bank must be able to explain why a connection was allowed or blocked.
  4. In which regions can a customer require its logs and inspected content to be stored and processed, and is that a contractual commitment?
    Banks may be required to keep logs and inspected content within a jurisdiction.
  5. Is customer traffic, log or content data used to train or tune AI or machine-learning models, and if so are those models shared across customers and can a customer opt out?
    Inspected traffic and logs contain sensitive bank data; use for model training must be understood.
  6. How are customer logs and any retained content encrypted at rest and in transit, and with what algorithms and key lengths?
    Stored logs and any retained content need protection at rest and in transit.
  7. Can a customer hold or manage the encryption keys for its stored data and for TLS inspection certificates, for example in its own HSM?
    Key control determines who can read stored data and TLS inspection keys.
  8. Which third-party AI model providers, if any, does the Zero Trust Exchange depend on, and for which features?
