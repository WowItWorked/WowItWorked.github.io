Palo Alto Networks' identity security platform, built on the CyberArk products it acquired in February 2026 and renamed Idira in May 2026. It covers privileged access management (credential vaulting and rotation, session isolation and recording, just-in-time and zero-standing-privilege access), endpoint privilege management, secrets management for applications and workloads, and controls for machine and AI-agent identities. Offered as SaaS and as self-hosted software.

How confidence is setIt follows the kind of evidence, not judgement.
  • The customer's own statement (high confidence at most): The organisation says so itself: its press release, website, annual report or a named executive on the record.
  • Regulatory or securities filing (high confidence at most): A filing by the customer or the vendor names the relationship.
  • Independent press (high confidence at most): Editorially independent reporting describes the deployment.
  • Joint announcement (medium confidence at most): A press release issued with the vendor, usually with a quote from the customer.
  • Vendor case study (medium confidence at most): The vendor's published customer story naming the organisation.
  • Vendor mention only (low confidence at most): A logo, a customer list or a passing mention by the vendor, with nothing from the customer.
  • An organisation that also holds a stake in the vendor is labelled “Investor and customer” and is never rated above medium.
  • A partnership is listed as a partner, not a customer.

Questions to ask the vendor (10)

Each is something that could not be established from public sources. Export them as a list to send, with empty columns for the vendor’s answer and the evidence supplied.

Export and share
Open as a question list
  1. Is customer data from Idira (including session recordings and audit data) used to train or tune any model, and if so is the model shared across customers and can the customer opt out?
    Idira includes AI features, and the parent lists several generative AI providers; a bank needs to know whether its data trains shared models.
  2. How does Idira explain AI-generated risk findings, permission recommendations and session summaries, and can each be traced to the underlying events?
    The platform uses AI to profile permissions and summarise sessions; a bank needs to know how those outputs can be explained and checked.
  3. What standard reports or evidence packs does Idira produce for auditors, and can a bank see examples?
    Banks need reports that auditors and examiners can use directly.
  4. How are vaulted credentials, secrets and session recordings encrypted in transit and at rest in Idira SaaS, and who holds the keys?
    Vaulted credentials and session recordings are among the most sensitive data a bank holds.
  5. Can a customer supply and control its own encryption keys for Idira SaaS services?
    Control of encryption keys limits exposure if the provider is compromised.
  6. Which model providers do Idira's AI features (including the CORA AI assistant and session summaries) depend on, and in which regions do they process data?
