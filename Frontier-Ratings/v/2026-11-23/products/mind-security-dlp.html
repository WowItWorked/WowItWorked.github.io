How confidence is setIt follows the kind of evidence, not judgement.
  • The customer's own statement (high confidence at most): The organisation says so itself: its press release, website, annual report or a named executive on the record.
  • Regulatory or securities filing (high confidence at most): A filing by the customer or the vendor names the relationship.
  • Independent press (high confidence at most): Editorially independent reporting describes the deployment.
  • Joint announcement (medium confidence at most): A press release issued with the vendor, usually with a quote from the customer.
  • Vendor case study (medium confidence at most): The vendor's published customer story naming the organisation.
  • Vendor mention only (low confidence at most): A logo, a customer list or a passing mention by the vendor, with nothing from the customer.
  • An organisation that also holds a stake in the vendor is labelled “Investor and customer” and is never rated above medium.
  • A partnership is listed as a partner, not a customer.

Questions to ask the vendor (25)

Each is something that could not be established from public sources. Export them as a list to send, with empty columns for the vendor’s answer and the evidence supplied.

Export and share
Open as a question list
  1. Has MIND made any workforce reductions since it was founded?
    Layoffs can signal financial stress or reduced support capacity.
  2. Has MIND been the subject of any regulatory investigation or enforcement action?
    Enforcement history bears on a supplier's conduct risk.
  3. Is MIND party to any material litigation, pending or resolved?
    Material litigation can affect a supplier's finances and continuity.
  4. Has MIND had any security incident affecting its own systems or customer data, and how was it disclosed?
    A DLP supplier sees the bank's most sensitive content, so its own incident record matters.
  5. Has MIND lost any major customer in the last two years, and why?
    Loss of major customers can indicate product or service problems.
  6. Have any founders or senior executives left MIND, or been subject to adverse findings?
    Leadership instability affects delivery and support.
  7. What is MIND's pricing basis (per user, per endpoint or by data volume), and what is the list price for each module?
    Without a pricing basis a bank cannot estimate cost at enterprise scale.
  8. Does MIND mask, redact or tokenise sensitive values, or only detect and block their movement?
    Masking lets data stay usable while reducing exposure.
Show the other 17
  1. What explanation and evidence does MIND record for each decision made by its classification engine and AI agents?
    When an AI agent blocks an action or closes an incident, the bank must be able to see why.
  2. Does MIND offer an API, webhook or streaming export of incidents and audit records?
    Events must reach the bank's own monitoring to be useful in investigations.
  3. Which SIEM products does MIND integrate with, and what event types are sent?
    SIEM integration is needed to correlate data-loss events with other security telemetry.
  4. In which regions can a MIND tenant be hosted, and can a customer choose?
    A bank may need content inspection and stored events to stay in a chosen jurisdiction.
  5. Can MIND provide its current sub-processor list with purposes and locations?
    Sub-processors are fourth parties that may handle the bank's data.
  6. Is customer content used to train or tune MIND's AI models, and are those models shared across customers?
    Classification models that learn from one customer's content could expose it to others.
  7. How does MIND encrypt customer data at rest and in transit?
    Encryption at rest and in transit is a baseline control.
  8. Can a customer supply and control the encryption keys for its MIND tenant?
    Customer-held keys limit the supplier's access to stored data.
  9. Has MIND had any breach of customer data?
    Past breaches indicate how well the supplier protects what it holds.
  10. Does MIND publish service status and incident history, and what incidents occurred in the last 24 months?
    Outage history shows how reliable the service is.
  11. Are any of the AI models used by MIND provided or hosted by third parties, and which?
    Third-party model providers are fourth parties that may process the bank's data.
  12. What support tiers, hours and response targets does MIND offer?
    Support hours and response targets determine how quickly a blocking fault is resolved.
  13. On termination, what data can a customer export from MIND and in what format, and how is stored data deleted?
    A bank needs to know how policies and incident history are exported and agents removed.
  14. In which countries does MIND process and store customer data?
    Processing location determines which laws apply to the bank's data.
  15. From which countries do MIND support staff access customer environments?
    Support staff location determines who can access the bank's data.
  16. Can MIND export incidents and audit records by API or stream, which SIEM products does it support, and how long are records kept?
    Without knowing whether events can be exported or sent to a SIEM, the product cannot be placed on the observability scale.
  17. Where is customer data processed and stored, from which countries is support delivered, and can the customer choose the hosting region?
    Processing, hosting and support locations were not found, so country exposure cannot be placed on the scale.
