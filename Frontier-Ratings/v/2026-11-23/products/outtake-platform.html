A digital risk protection platform in which AI agents search domains, social and video platforms, messaging apps, app stores, adverts and dark web sources for impersonation of a customer's brands, executives, locations and products, link related assets into a campaign view, and submit takedown requests. Brand, executive, location and product protection, narrative intelligence, the Recon Agent and Intake are sold as solutions on the one platform. The vendor says it complements, and does not replace, transaction monitoring and fraud detection.

How confidence is setIt follows the kind of evidence, not judgement.
  • The customer's own statement (high confidence at most): The organisation says so itself: its press release, website, annual report or a named executive on the record.
  • Regulatory or securities filing (high confidence at most): A filing by the customer or the vendor names the relationship.
  • Independent press (high confidence at most): Editorially independent reporting describes the deployment.
  • Joint announcement (medium confidence at most): A press release issued with the vendor, usually with a quote from the customer.
  • Vendor case study (medium confidence at most): The vendor's published customer story naming the organisation.
  • Vendor mention only (low confidence at most): A logo, a customer list or a passing mention by the vendor, with nothing from the customer.
  • An organisation that also holds a stake in the vendor is labelled “Investor and customer” and is never rated above medium.
  • A partnership is listed as a partner, not a customer.

Export and share
Open as a question list
  1. Has Outtake made any redundancies or workforce reductions in the last 24 months, and if so how many roles and in which teams?
    Layoffs can signal financial strain and reduce support capacity.
  2. Has Outtake been subject to any regulatory investigation, enforcement action or order in any jurisdiction?
    Regulatory action against a supplier is a third-party risk a bank must assess.
  3. Is Outtake a party to any pending or recently resolved litigation, and what is each matter about?
    Material litigation can affect a supplier's finances and its ability to deliver.
  4. Has Outtake had any security incident or data breach affecting its systems or customer data, and how was it disclosed?
    A bank needs to know whether a supplier holding its brand and investigation data has been breached.
  5. Has Outtake lost any enterprise or financial services customer in the last 24 months, and why?
    Loss of large customers can indicate product or service problems.
  6. Have there been any departures of founders or senior executives, or any governance concerns, at Outtake in the last 24 months?
    Leadership instability or misconduct affects a supplier's reliability.
  7. What activity logs does the Outtake platform keep of agent actions and user actions, and how can the customer view and export them?
    A bank needs logs of what the platform's agents did on its behalf, including takedown requests sent in its name.
  8. Does the platform keep an audit trail of every takedown request, approval and configuration change, and is it tamper-evident?
    Takedown requests are sent to third parties in the bank's name, so the bank needs a complete record of who or what authorised each one.
Show the other 16
  1. Does the platform offer a documented API or streaming export of findings, cases and takedown records, and where is it documented?
    The bank will want findings and takedown records in its own tools.
  2. In which regions can a customer choose to have its Outtake data stored and processed?
    Some banks must keep data in a chosen jurisdiction.
  3. Which sub-processors does Outtake use for the platform, for what purpose, and where is each located?

  4. Is customer data, including analyst feedback and case data, used to train or tune models or detection logic shared across customers, and can a customer opt out?

    Encryption in transit and at rest is a baseline control.
  6. Does the Outtake platform support customer-managed encryption keys?
    Some banks require control of encryption keys.
  7. Has the Outtake platform had any breach of customer data, and how were customers notified?
    Past breaches indicate how the vendor protects and discloses.
  8. Does Outtake publish a status page or incident history for the platform, and what outages occurred in the last 24 months?
    Outage history shows how reliable the service is.
  9. Which cloud providers and regions host the Outtake platform?
    Cloud hosting is a concentration risk the bank must map.
  10. What support plans, hours, channels and response-time commitments does Outtake offer for the platform?
    The bank needs to know support hours, channels and response commitments.
  11. On termination, in what format can a customer export its entity registry, cases, evidence and takedown records, and when is its data deleted?

  12. From which countries do Outtake staff and contractors provide support and access customer data?

    Independent attestations are the main evidence of a supplier's control environment.

    The site shows an AICPA SOC badge, but the report type, period and scope could not be read, so the attestation cannot be placed on the scale.
