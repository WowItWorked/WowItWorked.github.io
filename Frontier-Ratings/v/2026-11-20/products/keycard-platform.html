Identity and access service for AI agents. It gives each agent and workload an identity, checks each credential request against policy written in Cedar, and issues short-lived, scoped tokens in place of static keys. It federates the customer's existing identity provider for the people agents act for. It is not a workforce sign-on product. The vendor opened it in early access in October 2025 and now sells self-serve and enterprise plans; no general-availability announcement was found.

How confidence is setIt follows the kind of evidence, not judgement.
  • The customer's own statement (high confidence at most): The organisation says so itself: its press release, website, annual report or a named executive on the record.
  • Regulatory or securities filing (high confidence at most): A filing by the customer or the vendor names the relationship.
  • Independent press (high confidence at most): Editorially independent reporting describes the deployment.
  • Joint announcement (medium confidence at most): A press release issued with the vendor, usually with a quote from the customer.
  • Vendor case study (medium confidence at most): The vendor's published customer story naming the organisation.
  • Vendor mention only (low confidence at most): A logo, a customer list or a passing mention by the vendor, with nothing from the customer.
  • An organisation that also holds a stake in the vendor is labelled “Investor and customer” and is never rated above medium.
  • A partnership is listed as a partner, not a customer.

Questions to ask the vendor (16)

Each is something that could not be established from public sources. Export them as a list to send, with empty columns for the vendor’s answer and the evidence supplied.

Export and share
Open as a question list
  1. How many people does Keycard employ, and how many work in engineering and support?
    Headcount indicates whether a young vendor can support an enterprise deployment.
  2. Has Keycard reduced its workforce since it was founded, and if so when and by how much?
    Workforce reductions at an early-stage vendor bear on its ability to support the product.
  3. Has Keycard been subject to any regulatory investigation, order or penalty?
    Regulatory action against an identity vendor bears on a bank's own supervisory standing.
  4. Is Keycard party to any pending or resolved litigation, and what is it about?
    Material litigation can affect a young vendor's finances and focus.
  5. Has Keycard had any security incident affecting its service or customer data, and how was it handled?
    A credential-issuing service is a high-value target; its incident record matters.
  6. Has Keycard lost any notable customer, and why?
    Customer departures can signal product or service problems.
  7. Have there been any departures or disputes among Keycard's founders or executives?
    Leadership instability is a risk at an early-stage vendor.
  8. In which regions does Keycard's multi-tenant cloud store and process customer data, and can a customer choose the region?
    A bank needs to know where credentials, tokens and logs are stored and whether it can choose the region.
Show the other 8
  1. Does Keycard use customer data, tokens, policies or audit events to train or tune any model, and if so is that model shared across customers?
    A bank must know whether its data is used to train models.
  2. Has the Keycard service ever had a breach or unauthorised access to customer data or credentials?
    Past breaches show how the product and vendor behave under attack.
  3. Where does Keycard publish service status and incident history, and what has uptime been over the last 12 months?
    Outage history shows whether the availability commitment is met in practice.
  4. Does any Keycard feature send customer data to a third-party AI model provider, and which one?
    Dependence on a third-party AI model provider is a fourth-party risk.
  5. On termination, what policies, configuration and audit data can a customer export from Keycard, in what format, and for how long is it retained?
    A bank must be able to leave the service and take its configuration and logs with it.
  6. In which countries is customer data processed by Keycard and each of its sub-processors?
    Processing locations determine which laws apply to the bank's data.
  7. From which countries do Keycard staff provide support, and can support access be limited to chosen jurisdictions?
    Support staff with access to a credential service are a jurisdictional exposure.
  8. Where is the Keycard multi-tenant service hosted, where are support staff located, and can both be limited to jurisdictions the bank chooses?
    Country risk cannot be placed without knowing where the hosted service processes data and where support is provided from.
