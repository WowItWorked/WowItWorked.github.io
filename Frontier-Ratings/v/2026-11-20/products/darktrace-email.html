Email security product that learns each organisation's and user's normal communication behaviour and acts on messages that deviate from it, rather than relying on known-bad signatures. It runs alongside the native protection in Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, and extends to outbound data loss, account takeover response, Microsoft Teams and other collaboration tools, DMARC management and adaptive awareness training. Darktrace says the product launched in 2019.

How confidence is setIt follows the kind of evidence, not judgement.
  • The customer's own statement (high confidence at most): The organisation says so itself: its press release, website, annual report or a named executive on the record.
  • Regulatory or securities filing (high confidence at most): A filing by the customer or the vendor names the relationship.
  • Independent press (high confidence at most): Editorially independent reporting describes the deployment.
  • Joint announcement (medium confidence at most): A press release issued with the vendor, usually with a quote from the customer.
  • Vendor case study (medium confidence at most): The vendor's published customer story naming the organisation.
  • Vendor mention only (low confidence at most): A logo, a customer list or a passing mention by the vendor, with nothing from the customer.
  • An organisation that also holds a stake in the vendor is labelled “Investor and customer” and is never rated above medium.
  • A partnership is listed as a partner, not a customer.

Export and share
Open as a question list
  1. Has Darktrace been subject to any regulatory investigation, enforcement action or order in the last five years?
    Regulatory action against a supplier is a due-diligence finding in its own right.
  2. Is Darktrace party to any material litigation, pending or resolved in the last five years?
    Material litigation can affect a supplier's finances and its ability to keep serving the bank.
  3. Has Darktrace had any security incident affecting customer data or its production service, and what was disclosed to customers?
    A security product supplier's own incident record bears directly on the bank's exposure.
  4. Has Darktrace lost any large or regulated customer in the last two years, and why?
    Departures of large customers can signal product or service problems.
  5. Has Darktrace reduced its workforce in the last two years, and by how much?
    Workforce reductions can affect support and product development.
  6. What is the per-mailbox price of each Darktrace / EMAIL tier, and what is the minimum term?
    Budgeting needs at least an order of magnitude.
  7. What does Darktrace / EMAIL log for each message and action, and how long are those logs kept?
    The bank needs a record of what the product did to each message.
  8. Does Darktrace / EMAIL keep an audit log of administrator and autonomous actions, and can it be exported?
    Administrator changes to an autonomous email control must be traceable.
Show the other 6
  1. How does Darktrace encrypt Darktrace / EMAIL customer data in transit and at rest?
    Encryption of stored email data is a baseline control.
  2. Does Darktrace / EMAIL support customer-managed encryption keys?
    Some banks require control of encryption keys.
  3. Has Darktrace had any breach of customer data in its email service?
    Past breaches indicate how the vendor protects customer data.
  4. Does Darktrace publish service incidents for Darktrace / EMAIL, and what incidents occurred in the last 12 months?
    Outage history shows how reliable the service is.
  5. What data can a customer export from Darktrace / EMAIL at exit, in what format, and how long is data kept after termination?
    The bank must be able to leave with its data and without disruption to mail flow.
  6. Can Darktrace provide documentation of the audit trail, log export and SIEM integrations of Darktrace / EMAIL?
    The bank needs to know what record the product keeps and what it can export.
