Data security platform that discovers and classifies sensitive data across cloud, SaaS, data warehouses, AI knowledge stores and on-premises file shares, maps which people, service accounts and AI agents can reach it, and applies labels, access revocation and browser-based controls on data sent to AI tools. Scanning runs inside the customer's own environment.

How confidence is setIt follows the kind of evidence, not judgement.
  • The customer's own statement (high confidence at most): The organisation says so itself: its press release, website, annual report or a named executive on the record.
  • Regulatory or securities filing (high confidence at most): A filing by the customer or the vendor names the relationship.
  • Independent press (high confidence at most): Editorially independent reporting describes the deployment.
  • Joint announcement (medium confidence at most): A press release issued with the vendor, usually with a quote from the customer.
  • Vendor case study (medium confidence at most): The vendor's published customer story naming the organisation.
  • Vendor mention only (low confidence at most): A logo, a customer list or a passing mention by the vendor, with nothing from the customer.
  • An organisation that also holds a stake in the vendor is labelled “Investor and customer” and is never rated above medium.
  • A partnership is listed as a partner, not a customer.

Questions to ask the vendor (13)

Each is something that could not be established from public sources. Export them as a list to send, with empty columns for the vendor’s answer and the evidence supplied.

Export and share
Open as a question list
  1. Has Sentra made any workforce reductions in the last three years?
    Layoffs can signal financial stress or reduced support capacity.
  2. Has Sentra been the subject of any regulatory investigation or enforcement action?
    Enforcement history bears on a supplier's conduct risk.
  3. Is Sentra party to any material litigation, pending or resolved in the last three years?
    Material litigation can affect a supplier's finances and continuity.
  4. Has Sentra had any security incident affecting its own systems or customer metadata, and how was it disclosed?
    A data security supplier's own incident record matters because it holds metadata about the bank's sensitive data.
  5. Has Sentra lost any major customer in the last two years, and why?
    Loss of major customers can indicate product or service problems.
  6. Have any founders or senior executives left Sentra in the last two years, or been subject to adverse findings?
    Leadership instability affects delivery and support.
  7. What explanation does Sentra give for each classification decision made by its AI models, and can it be exported?
    A bank needs to know why a file was classified as sensitive in order to trust and challenge automated labels.
  8. Is any customer data or metadata used to train or tune Sentra's classification models, and are those models shared across customers?
    Classification models that learn from one customer's data could expose it to others.
Show the other 5
  1. Can a customer supply and control the keys that encrypt the metadata Sentra stores about its data?
    Customer-held keys limit the supplier's access to stored metadata.
  2. Has Sentra had any breach of customer metadata or of its scanners' access credentials?
    Past breaches indicate how well the supplier protects what it holds.
  3. Does Sentra publish service status and incident history, and what incidents occurred in the last 24 months?
    Outage history shows how reliable the service is.
  4. Which AI models classify data in Sentra, are any provided by third parties, and where do they run?
    Third-party model providers are fourth parties that may process the bank's data.
  5. On termination, in what format can a customer export its data inventory and classifications, and how are scanner roles and stored metadata removed?
    A bank needs to know how it gets its inventory and labels out and how scanners are removed.
