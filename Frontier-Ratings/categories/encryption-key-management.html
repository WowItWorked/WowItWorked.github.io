Why it matters to a bank

  • Whoever holds the keys can read the data, including a cloud provider
  • Keys are scattered across systems with no central control
  • Hardware and algorithms must be replaced for the post-quantum transition

Where the category is heading

  1. 2026-10-16

    Microsoft made external key management for Azure Key Vault Managed HSM generally available in all Azure public regions, according to an Azure Updates notice dated 16 October 2026. The feature lets Azure services that support customer-managed keys use key-encryption keys held in an HSM the customer operates outside Microsoft's infrastructure; access has to be enabled through the customer's Microsoft account team. Azure Updates notice, mirrored by mc.merill.net (other, retrieved 2026-11-20, opens in new tab)Microsoft Azure Blog (vendor, retrieved 2026-11-20, opens in new tab)

  2. 2026-09-21

    NIST's Cryptographic Module Validation Program states that US federal agencies could accept FIPS 140-2 validated modules for new systems only through 21 September 2026; after that date the programme moves those modules to its Historical list, where they may continue to be used in existing systems only. NIST Computer Security Resource Center (regulator or standards body, retrieved 2026-11-20, opens in new tab)

  3. 2026-07-07

    Keyfactor, which sells tools for discovering cryptographic assets and managing machine identities, received an investment of more than $1 billion led by Summit Partners, with existing backers Insight Partners and Sixth Street Growth keeping significant stakes. Stated uses include product development, geographic expansion and possible acquisitions. SecurityWeek (independent press, retrieved 2026-11-20, opens in new tab)

  4. 2025-10-01

    Utimaco sold its lawful interception unit, Utimaco Telecom Solutions, to investment firms INVISION and PINOVA; the sale completed on 30 September 2025 and the price was not disclosed. Utimaco said it would concentrate on its information and data security business. Utimaco (vendor, retrieved 2026-11-20, opens in new tab)

    LS2 HSM module validated to FIPS 140-3 Level 3 with ML-KEM and ML-DSA

    Marvell and Lumentum sign definitive agreement to combine in an all-stock deal; not yet closed

    Marvell announced in a press release dated 2026-11-02 that it and Lumentum had signed a definitive agreement, approved by both boards, under which Marvell will acquire Lumentum in an all-stock transaction: each Lumentum share converts into 4.500 Marvell shares, which Marvell put at an implied enterprise value of US$115 billion for Lumentum. Existing Marvell stockholders would own about 67% of the combined company and Lumentum stockholders about 33%, and Matt Murphy would remain chairman and chief executive. Marvell expects closing in the first half of calendar 2028, subject to approval by both companies' stockholders, regulatory approvals and other customary conditions.
