Why it matters to a bank

  • Malware, phishing and ransomware reach their servers through DNS lookups that no other control inspects
  • Data is carried out of the bank inside DNS queries, which firewalls and proxies usually let through
  • The bank cannot tell which device or user made a suspicious lookup, so investigations stall

Where the category is heading

  1. 2026-11-23

    Government guidance treats this as a baseline control. The US agencies NSA and CISA published joint guidance in March 2021 on choosing a protective DNS service, describing it as a defence that blocks known-malicious domains and yields query logs for incident response, and CISA has run its own protective DNS resolver for federal civilian agencies since 2022. Commercial products do the same core job in different places. Akamai's service is a cloud DNS firewall with clients for devices off the network; Cloudflare applies DNS policies by user, location and category inside its zero-trust platform; BlueCat and Infoblox sell it alongside their DNS, DHCP and IP address management products, and Infoblox enforces policy on the resolvers the organisation already runs as well as in the cloud. The direction is from blocklists towards analysing query behaviour to catch tunnelling and algorithm-generated domains, blocking newly registered or suspicious domains before they are used, and feeding DNS evidence into security operations tools.
