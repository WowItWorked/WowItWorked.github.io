Why it matters to a bank

  • An attacker who is already inside moves between internal systems unseen, because perimeter and endpoint tools do not watch traffic between them
  • Unmanaged, legacy and operational devices that cannot run an endpoint agent go unmonitored
  • After an incident the bank cannot reconstruct what happened on the network because no traffic record or packets were kept

Where the category is heading

  1. 2026-11-22

    The product pages of four vendors in this market describe the same core: passive sensors on premises and in public cloud, behavioural or machine-learning detection alongside signatures and threat intelligence, a record of network activity for investigation, and hand-off to SIEM, SOAR and endpoint tools. They differ at the edges. ExtraHop decrypts traffic out of band and Darktrace says it analyses both encrypted and decrypted traffic, while Vectra AI describes analysing encrypted traffic by behaviour and metadata without reading payloads. Vectra AI adds identity and cloud log sources to network data, Corelight builds on the open-source Zeek and Suricata projects, and Darktrace describes containing threats itself as well as through other tools. All four now present AI-assisted or agent-driven triage and investigation, and Corelight and ExtraHop offer Model Context Protocol servers so that AI agents can query network evidence. Sensor speed is rising: ExtraHop announced a 400 Gbps sensor in August 2026.     ExtraHop announces a 400 Gbps sensor for RevealX

    On 25 August 2026 ExtraHop announced a sensor for RevealX that it says analyses data-centre traffic at 400 Gbps at line rate, with general availability expected in the fourth quarter of 2026. The throughput figure is the company's own; no independent measurement was found. ExtraHop (vendor, retrieved 2026-11-22, opens in new tab)

    ExtraHop RevealX Federal receives FedRAMP authorisation at Moderate impact

    The FedRAMP Marketplace lists ExtraHop RevealX Federal as FedRAMP Authorized at Moderate impact, with an agency authorisation dated 15 September 2025 and Schellman Compliance as assessor. The entry describes it as a SaaS offering hosted in AWS US-East regions. ExtraHop announced the authorisation on 7 October 2025.     ExtraHop names Rob Greer chief executive; Greg Clark becomes executive chairman

    Bain Capital Private Equity and Crosspoint Capital Partners agree to acquire ExtraHop for US$900 million

    On 8 June 2021 ExtraHop said it had agreed to be acquired by Bain Capital Private Equity and Crosspoint Capital Partners in a transaction valued at US$900 million, expected to close that summer. The acquirers' release said the co-founders would stay in their roles and remain significant investors. GeekWire reported the company had raised US$61.6 million before the deal and would keep operating under its own brand. Later press reports describe the company as owned by the two firms.
