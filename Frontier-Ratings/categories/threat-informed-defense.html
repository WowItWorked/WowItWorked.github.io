Why it matters to a bank

  • The bank cannot show which attacker techniques its existing tools actually detect or block
  • Security spend goes to overlapping tools while gaps against relevant threat groups stay open
  • Threat intelligence is read but not turned into detection and control changes

Where the category is heading

  1. 2026-11-22

    MITRE's Center for Threat-Informed Defense describes the practice as using known adversary behaviour relevant to an organisation's threat model to assess and improve its defences, and publishes free mappings from security controls to ATT&CK techniques. Commercial products apply this to a customer's own tools: Tidal Cyber overlays a defensive stack on a threat profile and takes in results from attack-simulation tools; CardinalOps measures detection coverage against adversary behaviour and finds broken rules; Reach Security looks for misconfigured or underused controls and recommends changes. The market is converging with neighbours: Cribl announced in July 2026 that it is acquiring CardinalOps, and vendors here are adding vulnerability and asset context and AI assistance. Recent events

  1. 2026-09-29 · launch Cribl
    Cribl launches Cribl Detect, a SIEM that includes detection posture management

    Cribl announced Cribl Detect on 29 September 2026 and said it was available to Cribl.Cloud customers. The release lists detection posture management, which maps existing detections to MITRE ATT&CK and shows coverage gaps, broken or noisy rules and missing telemetry, alongside in-stream detections, federated investigation and AI-assisted triage. TechTarget reported that the product combines technology from the CardinalOps and Radiant Security deals and is not yet supported on premises or in self-managed environments. Cribl's documentation labels every Cribl Detect page as Preview and says the feature is not recommended for production environments.   3. 2026-07-31 · launch Tidal Cyber
    Tidal Cyber adds a Model Context Protocol server for use with AI assistants

    In a release dated 31 July 2026 and published on 3 August 2026, Tidal Cyber announced a Model Context Protocol server that lets approved AI assistants query the platform's threat profiles, coverage maps and recommendations in natural language, with authenticated, tenant-scoped access. It cites ChatGPT, Claude and Microsoft Copilot as examples of such assistants and says the capability is included in the platform. Tidal Cyber (vendor, retrieved 2026-11-22, opens in new tab)

  4. 2026-07-14 · acquisition Cribl
    Cribl announces the acquisition of detection-coverage firm CardinalOps

    Cribl announced on 14 July 2026 that it was acquiring CardinalOps, a company founded in 2020 whose platform measured an organisation's detection coverage against adversary behaviour and found and repaired broken or noisy detection rules in other vendors' SIEM and EDR tools. Terms were not disclosed. BankInfoSecurity reported that CardinalOps had 35 staff and had raised US$24 million; TechTarget reported Cribl's chief executive saying about 20 security engineers and the intellectual property would move to Cribl. Cribl said it would open a Tel Aviv office for the team. No separate statement that the transaction had closed was found; a Cribl release of 19 August 2026 refers to it as an acquisition made in July, and when checked for this record cardinalops.com redirected permanently to cribl.io. The technology was then built into Cribl Detect.   6. 2026-05-13 · launch Tidal Cyber
    Tidal Cyber separates its own threat intelligence from MITRE ATT&CK content in the platform

    Tidal Cyber announced on 13 May 2026 a new architecture, aligned with ATT&CK version 19, that keeps ATT&CK as the technique-level structure and presents the company's own intelligence as procedure-level content with source attribution. The release says users can bring in vulnerability data and see its effect on procedures and attack likelihood. It gave no availability details.
