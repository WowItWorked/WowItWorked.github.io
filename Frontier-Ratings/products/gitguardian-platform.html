Secrets security platform that finds hardcoded credentials (API keys, tokens, passwords, certificates) in source repositories and their history, CI/CD pipelines, container and package registries, collaboration tools and developer machines, checks whether each is still valid, and manages the resulting incidents through to revocation. It also monitors public GitHub for an organisation's leaked credentials, inventories non-human identities across vaults and cloud identity services, and plants decoy credentials (honeytokens). The ggshield command-line tool blocks secrets before commit or push and in AI coding tools. It is sold as a hosted service in US or EU regions or self-hosted on Kubernetes. It does not analyse code for vulnerabilities, scan dependencies or test running applications.

How confidence is setIt follows the kind of evidence, not judgement.
  • The customer's own statement (high confidence at most): The organisation says so itself: its press release, website, annual report or a named executive on the record.
  • Regulatory or securities filing (high confidence at most): A filing by the customer or the vendor names the relationship.
  • Independent press (high confidence at most): Editorially independent reporting describes the deployment.
  • Joint announcement (medium confidence at most): A press release issued with the vendor, usually with a quote from the customer.
  • Vendor case study (medium confidence at most): The vendor's published customer story naming the organisation.
  • Vendor mention only (low confidence at most): A logo, a customer list or a passing mention by the vendor, with nothing from the customer.
  • An organisation that also holds a stake in the vendor is labelled “Investor and customer” and is never rated above medium.
  • A partnership is listed as a partner, not a customer.

    Researcher discloses that a ggshield option turned off TLS certificate verification entirely

    An independent researcher reported on the oss-security mailing list on 2025-11-17 that the --allow-self-signed option of ggshield, GitGuardian's command-line scanner, disabled all TLS certificate verification in versions up to 1.43.0, which could expose scanned content and API credentials to interception; no CVE was assigned and no exploitation was reported. The post says the vendor at first called the behaviour intentional. GitGuardian's release notes for ggshield 1.44.1 stated that the old option name was misleading, added an explicit --insecure option with warnings, and deprecated the old name.
