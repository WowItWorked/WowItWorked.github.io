Autonomous offensive security platform. Large numbers of coordinated AI agents, working under a control layer and with human red-team oversight, discover an organisation's exposed assets, attack them across external infrastructure, web applications and internal networks, and chain individual weaknesses into attack paths that have been shown to work. Customers receive the proven paths, their potential impact and prioritised remediation guidance. The vendor has no separate name for the platform; its assessment offering is called Armadin Red, and the technology is also sold as an external assessment through Palo Alto Networks Unit 42 and through CrowdStrike. Public material is press releases, partner pages and press reports; no technical documentation could be read.

How confidence is setIt follows the kind of evidence, not judgement.
  • The customer's own statement (high confidence at most): The organisation says so itself: its press release, website, annual report or a named executive on the record.
  • Regulatory or securities filing (high confidence at most): A filing by the customer or the vendor names the relationship.
  • Independent press (high confidence at most): Editorially independent reporting describes the deployment.
  • Joint announcement (medium confidence at most): A press release issued with the vendor, usually with a quote from the customer.
  • Vendor case study (medium confidence at most): The vendor's published customer story naming the organisation.
  • Vendor mention only (low confidence at most): A logo, a customer list or a passing mention by the vendor, with nothing from the customer.
  • An organisation that also holds a stake in the vendor is labelled “Investor and customer” and is never rated above medium.
  • A partnership is listed as a partner, not a customer.

Export and share
Open as a question list
  1. Has Armadin made any workforce reductions since it was founded, and if so when and how large?
    Workforce reductions can signal financial strain or a change in product priorities.
  2. Has Armadin been subject to any regulatory investigation, enforcement action or order since it was founded?
    Regulatory action against a supplier bears on a bank's own supervisory position.
  3. Is Armadin party to any material litigation, including any dispute over intellectual property or the prior employment of its founders?
    Material litigation can affect a vendor's finances and its ability to deliver.
  4. Has Armadin had any security incident affecting its own systems, its attack tooling or customer findings, and has any test it ran caused unintended disruption at a customer?
    A vendor that runs live attacks inside a bank's network holds sensitive findings and access; an incident at the vendor would expose them.
  5. Has Armadin lost any significant customer or channel partner since launch, and why?
    Loss of large customers can indicate product or service problems.
  6. Have any founders or senior executives left Armadin since launch, and how does the board manage the positions its chief executive and directors hold at investors and at partner companies?
    Leadership instability or conflicts of interest can affect delivery and governance.
  7. What is Armadin's pricing metric and list price for continuous testing and for a one-off assessment such as Armadin Red, bought directly and through CrowdStrike or Palo Alto Networks Unit 42?
    Pricing determines total cost and whether continuous testing is affordable across the estate.
  8. How is the Armadin platform deployed for internal-network testing (vendor-hosted service, software in the customer's cloud, or an appliance or agent on the customer's network), and where do the agents and their findings reside?
    How the product is delivered decides where attack tooling runs, what access it holds inside the bank's network and where findings are stored.
Show the other 21
  1. What audit trail does Armadin give the customer of each agent action, the control-layer decisions that allowed or blocked it and any human approvals, and how long is it retained?
    A bank must be able to show after the fact exactly what an autonomous attacker did inside its network, when, and under whose approval.
  2. Can findings, attack paths and agent activity logs be exported through an API or stream, and in what formats?
    Export of findings and activity lets the bank feed its own risk and monitoring systems.
  3. Which SIEM products does Armadin integrate with, what does it send them, and what data passes between the platform and CrowdStrike Falcon?
    The security operations centre needs to tell sanctioned test traffic from a real attack and to correlate the two.
  4. What reports does Armadin produce that are suitable for auditors and supervisors, for example for threat-led penetration testing requirements, and can a sample be provided?
    Banks must evidence testing programmes and remediation to auditors and supervisors.
  5. In which countries and regions can findings and reconnaissance data be stored, and can the customer choose?
    Findings describe exactly how to break into the bank; where they are stored matters.
  6. Can Armadin provide its current sub-processor list with purposes and locations?
    Sub-processors receive or host customer data and are fourth parties to the bank.
  7. Is data gathered in a customer's environment, including findings and attack paths, used to train or tune models or playbooks that serve other customers, and can the customer opt out?
    The vendor trains its own models; a bank needs to know whether what the agents learn in its network is carried to other customers.
  8. How are findings, captured credentials and reconnaissance data encrypted at rest and in transit?
    Findings and any credentials obtained during testing must be protected at rest and in transit.
  9. Does the Armadin platform support customer-managed encryption keys?
    Customer-held keys limit the vendor's and its hosts' access to sensitive findings.
  10. Has Armadin had any breach involving customer findings or data?
    A breach of the vendor would expose proven attack paths into its customers.
  11. Has any Armadin campaign caused an outage, data loss or other unintended effect in a customer's production environment, and is there a status page or incident log?
    Autonomous exploitation against production systems can cause outages; a record of test-caused disruption is material.
  12. Which cloud providers host the Armadin platform and its agent fleet?
    Hosting providers are fourth parties and a concentration risk.
