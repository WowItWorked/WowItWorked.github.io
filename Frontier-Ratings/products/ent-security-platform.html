Endpoint agent, with a browser extension, that records what users and AI agents do across applications, browsers, AI tools and data movement, infers intent with small models running on the device, and intervenes before a risky action completes, from a prompt to the user up to a block. The vendor positions it for insider risk, data loss, AI usage control and last-mile threats such as social engineering, and as an addition to an existing EDR rather than a replacement.

How confidence is setIt follows the kind of evidence, not judgement.
  • The customer's own statement (high confidence at most): The organisation says so itself: its press release, website, annual report or a named executive on the record.
  • Regulatory or securities filing (high confidence at most): A filing by the customer or the vendor names the relationship.
  • Independent press (high confidence at most): Editorially independent reporting describes the deployment.
  • Joint announcement (medium confidence at most): A press release issued with the vendor, usually with a quote from the customer.
  • Vendor case study (medium confidence at most): The vendor's published customer story naming the organisation.
  • Vendor mention only (low confidence at most): A logo, a customer list or a passing mention by the vendor, with nothing from the customer.
  • An organisation that also holds a stake in the vendor is labelled “Investor and customer” and is never rated above medium.
  • A partnership is listed as a partner, not a customer.

Export and share
Open as a question list
  1. Has Ent been subject to any regulatory investigation, enforcement action or order in the last five years?
    Regulatory action against a supplier is a due-diligence finding in its own right.
  2. Is Ent party to any material litigation, pending or resolved in the last five years?
    Material litigation can affect a supplier's finances and its ability to keep serving the bank.
  3. Has Ent had any security incident affecting customer data or its production service, and what was disclosed to customers?
    A security product supplier's own incident record bears directly on the bank's exposure.
  4. Has Ent lost any large or regulated customer in the last two years, and why?
    Departures of large customers can signal product or service problems.
  5. Have there been any departures, disputes or conduct findings involving Ent's senior leadership in the last two years?
    Leadership disputes or misconduct can disrupt a supplier.
  6. Has Ent reduced its workforce in the last two years, and by how much?
    Workforce reductions can affect support and product development.
  7. What is Ent's list price per endpoint per year, and what is the minimum contract?
    Budgeting needs at least an order of magnitude.
  8. Does Ent offer a staffed monitoring and response service for its platform, directly or through partners?
    Banks without a round-the-clock team need to know whether a staffed service exists.
Show the other 13
  1. Can the customer stage, delay and roll back Ent agent, model and policy updates by device group?
    A faulty agent or content update can take down the bank's own machines.
  2. Which operating system versions, including server and legacy versions, does the Ent agent support?
    Banks still run older and server operating systems.
  3. By what API, stream or file export can a customer take Ent's telemetry, timelines and intervention records out of the product?
    The bank needs the product's records in its own monitoring and archive.
  4. What reports or evidence packs does Ent produce for auditors, and can Ent share a third-party assurance report?
    Auditors and supervisors ask for evidence the tool can produce.
  5. In which regions can Ent's hosted service store customer data, and what stays on the endpoint or in the customer's own cloud?
    The bank must know where endpoint activity data is stored.
  6. Does Ent use customer telemetry to train or tune models, and if so are those models shared across customers?
    Endpoint activity records are sensitive; the bank must know whether they train shared models.
  7. How does Ent encrypt customer data in transit and at rest, and who holds the keys?
    Encryption of stored endpoint records is a baseline control.
  8. Does Ent support customer-managed encryption keys in its hosted service?
    Some banks require control of encryption keys.
  9. Has Ent had any breach of customer data since it began serving customers?
    Past breaches indicate how the vendor protects customer data.
  10. Does Ent publish service incidents, and what incidents has its hosted service had in the last 12 months?
    Outage history shows how reliable the service is.
  11. From which countries do Ent support and engineering staff access customer environments and data?
    Support staff may have access to customer data from other jurisdictions.
  12. Which third-party attestations (for example SOC 2 or ISO/IEC 27001) does Ent hold, and what is their scope?
    Independent assurance reports are a standard third-party risk requirement.

    The bank needs to know what visibility it gets into what the agent records and does.
