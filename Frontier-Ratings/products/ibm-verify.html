IBM's identity and access management product line. The cloud service provides single sign-on, multi-factor and passwordless authentication, risk-based access, account lifecycle management and access certification for workforce and customer identities; related Verify products cover on-premises access management, identity governance, privileged access, a directory and identity threat detection. The cloud service was called IBM Cloud Identity until June 2020 and IBM Security Verify until 2025.

How confidence is setIt follows the kind of evidence, not judgement.
  • The customer's own statement (high confidence at most): The organisation says so itself: its press release, website, annual report or a named executive on the record.
  • Regulatory or securities filing (high confidence at most): A filing by the customer or the vendor names the relationship.
  • Independent press (high confidence at most): Editorially independent reporting describes the deployment.
  • Joint announcement (medium confidence at most): A press release issued with the vendor, usually with a quote from the customer.
  • Vendor case study (medium confidence at most): The vendor's published customer story naming the organisation.
  • Vendor mention only (low confidence at most): A logo, a customer list or a passing mention by the vendor, with nothing from the customer.
  • An organisation that also holds a stake in the vendor is labelled “Investor and customer” and is never rated above medium.
  • A partnership is listed as a partner, not a customer.

  1. Which shareholders hold more than 5% of IBM's common stock, as reported in the most recent proxy statement?
    Large or controlling shareholders affect country and concentration risk assessments.
  2. Have any financial-services customers ended use of watsonx.governance or IBM's quantum-safe software in the last two years, and why?
    Public customer departures can indicate product or service problems.
  3. Have there been any departures, investigations or findings concerning IBM senior executives or directors in the last two years that the bank should know about?
    Leadership instability or misconduct findings affect confidence in a long-term supplier.
  4. Which sub-processors handle customer data for IBM Verify, for what purpose and in which countries, and how is the bank notified of changes?
    An identity service holds staff identities and credentials; the bank must know every third party involved.
  5. Is customer sign-in, device or behavioural data used to train or tune models shared across IBM Verify tenants, and can a customer opt out?
    Verify uses AI risk scoring and correlates threat patterns across tenants; the bank needs to know how its sign-in data is used.
  6. Can a customer supply and control its own encryption keys for its IBM Verify tenant?
    Banks often require control of the keys that protect identity data.
  7. Has the IBM Verify cloud service had any security breach affecting customer tenants in the last five years, and what was disclosed to customers?
    An identity provider is a prime target; past breaches matter to the bank's own exposure.
  8. What availability did IBM Verify achieve in each of the last 12 months in the bank's region, what service level and credits apply, and where is service status published?
    If single sign-on is down, staff cannot work; outage history and service levels matter.
Show the other 3
  1. On which cloud infrastructure does each IBM Verify region run?
    The bank needs to know which infrastructure provider its identity service depends on.
  2. Which AI models, and whose, sit behind IBM Verify's risk scoring, threat detection and other AI features?

    Support staff for an identity service can hold powerful access.
