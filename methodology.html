METHODOLOGY

How this site is produced

Emerging Tech Risk is a technology-risk research publication for financial-services risk, governance, and technology professionals. Everything on it is built from public information, and the code and data behind every project are open.

What is here

The site has two kinds of content. The Tech Risk Tracker is a daily-edition record of technology risk events affecting the financial sector — cybersecurity, fraud, artificial intelligence, third-party risk, operational resilience, data risk, physical security, and insider threat. The other projects are reference dashboards and briefings on frontier AI risk, cyber and technology risk quantification, and the post-quantum cryptography transition. They are point-in-time assessments of fast-moving areas and are labeled as prototypes.

Sources

The tracker draws only from an allowlist of public feeds and pages — financial regulators and supervisory bodies in the United States, United Kingdom, and European Union; law-enforcement and consumer-protection agencies; national cybersecurity authorities and standards bodies; payments and data-protection bodies; the National Hurricane Center for physical-resilience events; frontier-AI laboratories' own publications; and a small set of established technology-security news outlets. The allowlist is published in the tracker's repository as sources.json. Nothing enters the record from a source that is not on it, and every recorded item links to the source it came from.

Editorial process

Each edition is produced under a written editorial standard (EDITORIAL.md in the repository) and an update runbook that fixes the cadence and the steps. In brief: feeds are fetched on a weekday schedule; candidate items pass a relevance gate for financial-sector nexus; admitted items are drafted from the source's own title and excerpt, with citation limits per unit of text; and every new or changed sentence is re-read against its cited excerpts before publication. Lines that fail are fixed or cut, and the outcome of each run — feeds reached, items recorded, verification results — is logged in the repository. Corrections are appended, never silently edited.

Automation and human review

Fetching, drafting, and verification are automated under the standard above, with the process, prompts, and logs open for inspection. The standard itself, the source allowlist, and the assessments in each risk-area dossier are the responsibility of the author, who reviews the record and corrects it when it is wrong.

Independence

This is a personal research project. It is not affiliated with, sponsored by, or reviewed by any employer, former employer, or regulator, and it does not represent the views of any organization. There is no advertising, no sponsorship, and no commercial relationship with any source or vendor mentioned.

Limits

The record reflects what its allowlisted sources published, when they published it. It is not comprehensive, it is not real time, and an item's absence means nothing. Assessments are stated with an explicit confidence level and should be read as one practitioner's reading of the public record, not as advice for any particular organization.
